    The Story of Nick, Leon Levenstein, and Anna Lyudinovskova

    • 2019 - Conception: The relationship with the child’s mother began with a brief romantic affair, which led to conception. The relationship ended quickly.
    • 2019 - Pregnancy Notification: Nick learned of the pregnancy while in Paris. He traveled to St. Petersburg to discuss the future.
    • 2019 - Move to Malta and Birth of the Child: Anna and Nick moved to Malta in 2019. Leon was born on February 27, 2020. Tension in the relationship continued, leading to three court travel bans to prevent the mother from traveling to Russia with Leon.
    • 2020–2023 - Good relations and interactions with Leon: I always loved Leon and wanted to be with him. This was never an issue. I always loved my child and wanted to be in his life.
    • 2022 - Attempts to move and separation: I tried to move to Bali to please the mother, but faced difficulties, which led to the mother taking Leon to Russia in February 2022 without my consent or knowledge.
    • Attempts at reunification and legal issues: I faced legal and logistical challenges in attempts to reunite with Leon, which led to numerous meetings in Turkey, Finland, and Estonia, but constant issues with custody and travel arose.
    • Custody Incidents: I was involved in various international moves and legal maneuvers to maintain custody of Leon, including fleeing Estonia to Malta in 2022. Anna attacked me in the street and took Leon to the Russian Federation.
    • Subsequent stays in Turkey, Estonia, Finland, France, Indonesia: After numerous international moves, we agreed on a final meeting in France, which led to a temporary reunion but ultimately to separation when Anna returned to Russia with Leon in April 2023.
    • Anna’s Return to Bali in July 2023: After returning to Russia in April 2023, Anna made even more excessive demands for payments and living conditions so that Nick could see Leon. Meanwhile, Nick had a second child born in April and needed to spend time with the child, Max Levenstein, and his mother, Vanda Suwandi. Apparently, Anna had to leave her home in St. Petersburg as her brother, Andrei (the current temporary guardian), returned home drunk and aggressive. At some point, she became homeless and turned to Nick for housing and money. $48,000 in child support payments through direct transfers or expenses due to international financial restrictions on transactions with Russia. By the way, even in 2023, when there was no formal agreement, according to my accountant, I’m not sure what she did with them.

    Anna's Death and Leon's Abduction by His Uncle

    1. Anna came to Bali with Leon in July 2024, staying at my rented villa and hotel. I noticed a large lump in her chest and strongly advised her to see a doctor; she assured me everything was fine. Below is her photo from October 8, shortly before her presumed death.
    2. Anna demanded that my other child and partner leave Bali, which we couldn’t agree to, as they were citizens of Indonesia. Eventually, we arranged for child support for her move to Bangkok, and she left on November 5.
    3. I announced that I would visit Leon in Bangkok on November 11, but shortly after, Anna blocked all my contacts.
    4. According to third parties, Anna became seriously ill and left Bangkok around November 23.
    5. Andrei met her at the airport and took Leon when she was hospitalized.
    6. Andrei falsely claimed that she was alive during a period when we now know she was already dead. I found out about her death on December 21, by which time Andrei had already applied for temporary guardianship.
    7. Reasons I did not travel to Russia:
      • I didn’t know where Anna was.
      • I was afraid of Anna and her brother due to her statements.
      • Andrei did not disclose Leon's whereabouts and refused to return him.
      • I hired a law firm in December, but could not get details about the case and Leon's whereabouts until February.
    8. See the separate history of interactions with the custody authorities. Essentially, they put obstacles in my way, prevented me from seeing Leon, and handed all my documents to Andrei, the defendant in this case.

    Appellate Complaint to the Moscow City Court’s Civil Division

    Appellate Complaint to the Moscow City Court’s Civil Division, challenging the decision of the Basmanny District Court of Moscow issued on March 10, 2025.

    Nicholas Levenstein is appealing a March 10, 2025 decision by the Basmanny District Court of Moscow, which denied his request to regain custody of his son, Leon Levenstein (b. 2020), after the child’s mother passed away. The appeal, now filed with the Moscow City Court, argues that the lower court's decision was unlawful, unsubstantiated, and failed to evaluate key evidence.

    Nicholas claims he has consistently sought custody and provided documentation proving his parenting capabilities, stable housing in Malta, Indonesia, and the U.S., and emotional ties with his son. He alleges the authorities wrongfully granted guardianship to a third party (Andrey Malevany), ignoring international and Russian laws that prioritize biological parents. The appeal also criticizes the court’s reliance on irrelevant geopolitical concerns over the child’s best interests.

    Court Complaint Filed June 2025 Regarding Refusal to Register Criminal Report

    On June 2025, attorneys representing Nicholas Levenstein submitted a formal complaint to the Basmanny District Court of Moscow against the refusal of the Basmanny Investigative Department to register a crime report. The report, submitted in November 2024, accused Svetlana Istomina, Deputy Head of Guardianship Department No. 5, of exceeding official powers by placing Leon Levenstein under the guardianship of his uncle, despite the legal rights of his father.

    The complaint includes legal arguments referencing Article 286, Part 3(d) of the Russian Criminal Code and Constitutional Court precedents. It requests that the court declare the refusal unlawful, register the crime report, and initiate a proper criminal inquiry.
